This guide cuts through the confusion with specific, verified information for South African patients and clinicians.

## Ozempic (Semaglutide): The Pioneer

### What It Is
Ozempic is a once-weekly injectable GLP-1 receptor agonist manufactured by Novo Nordisk. It was approved by SAHPRA for the treatment of type 2 diabetes in South Africa and is available in pre-filled pens containing 0.25 mg, 0.5 mg, and 1 mg doses per injection.

### What SAHPRA Says
Ozempic is **registered for type 2 diabetes** to reduce blood sugar levels and to reduce the risk of major cardiovascular events in adults with T2D and known heart disease. **It is NOT registered for weight management in South Africa.**

However, physicians can prescribe it **off-label** for weight loss if they determine it is clinically appropriate for an individual patient. This is a common and legal practice, but it means the medication is not officially indicated for weight loss by SAHPRA.

### Pricing in South Africa
- **0.25/0.5 mg pen:** ~R2,700–R3,100 per month (4 doses)
- **1 mg pen:** ~R2,700–R3,100 per month (4 doses)
- **2 mg maintenance:** Requires two 1 mg pens per month = ~R5,400–R6,200

### Clinical Evidence for Weight Loss
The landmark **STEP-1 trial** (Wilding et al., NEJM 2021):
- 1,961 adults with overweight or obesity
- 68 weeks of treatment
- **Average weight loss: 14.9%** with semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly vs. 2.4% with placebo
- **86.4%** achieved ≥5% weight loss
- **69.1%** achieved ≥10% weight loss
- **50.5%** achieved ≥15% weight loss

The **STEP-5 trial** confirmed these results are maintained over 2 years of continuous treatment.

### Side Effects
**Common (first 4–8 weeks):**
- Nausea: 30–40%
- Diarrhea: 15–25%
- Vomiting: 10–20%
- Constipation: 10–15%
- Abdominal pain: 5–10%
- Fatigue: 5–10%

**Serious (rare):**
- Pancreatitis: ~0.3% (seek immediate care for severe abdominal pain)
- Gallbladder disease: ~2–3%
- Acute kidney injury (from dehydration due to vomiting)
- Hypoglycemia (when combined with insulin or sulfonylureas)

### Contraindications
- Personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC)
- Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN2)
- History of pancreatitis
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding
- Severe gastrointestinal disease (gastroparesis)

### Medical Aid Coverage
**Discovery Health:**
- For T2D: Covered under Chronic Illness Benefit for members on all plan types (excluding KeyCare) when clinical criteria are met.
- If criteria are not met: Funded from day-to-day benefits (medical savings account and above threshold).
- For weight loss: Not covered under Chronic Illness Benefit. May be funded from day-to-day benefits at the plan's discretion.

**Momentum:** Similar structure to Discovery. T2D coverage under chronic benefits. Weight management from day-to-day benefits.

## Mounjaro (Tirzepatide): The New Standard

### What It Is
Mounjaro is a once-weekly injectable dual agonist that activates both GLP-1 and GIP receptors. Manufactured by Eli Lilly and distributed in South Africa by Aspen Pharmacare under a marketing agreement for sub-Saharan Africa.

### What SAHPRA Says
Mounjaro is **registered for type 2 diabetes** in South Africa. It is available in single-dose pre-filled pens containing 2.5 mg, 5 mg, 7.5 mg, 10 mg, 12.5 mg, and 15 mg.

**Weight management indication:** Eli Lilly has submitted an application to SAHPRA for a chronic weight loss indication. This is **pending review** as of June 2026. Until approved, Mounjaro is officially indicated for T2D only, though off-label prescribing for weight loss is possible.

### Pricing in South Africa
- **2.5 mg pen:** R880
- **5 mg pen:** R880
- **10 mg pen:** R1,140
- **Monthly maintenance (10 mg × 4):** ~R4,600
- **Monthly maintenance (15 mg × 4):** ~R4,600–R5,000

### Clinical Evidence for Weight Loss
The **SURMOUNT-1 trial** (Jastreboff et al., NEJM 2022):
- 2,539 adults with obesity or overweight
- 72 weeks of treatment
- **5 mg dose:** 15% average weight loss
- **10 mg dose:** 19.5% average weight loss
- **15 mg dose:** 20.9% average weight loss

The **SURMOUNT-5 trial** (2024) — head-to-head vs. semaglutide:
- Tirzepatide: 20.2% weight loss
- Semaglutide: 13.7% weight loss
- **Advantage: 6.5 percentage points** for tirzepatide

### Why It Outperforms Semaglutide
Tirzepatide's dual mechanism explains the superior results:
- **GLP-1 receptor:** Reduces appetite, slows gastric emptying, improves insulin sensitivity (same as semaglutide)
- **GIP receptor:** Enhances the GLP-1 effect, may have independent effects on adipose tissue, and improves insulin secretion in response to meals

### Side Effects
**Common (first 4–8 weeks):**
- Nausea: 40–50% (higher than semaglutide)
- Diarrhea: 20–30%
- Vomiting: 15–20%
- Constipation: 10–15%
- Decreased appetite: 80–90% (this is the intended effect, but can be excessive)
- Fatigue: 10–15%

### Contraindications
Same as semaglutide: MTC/MEN2 history, pancreatitis, pregnancy, severe GI disease.

### Medical Aid Coverage
Same as Ozempic: T2D coverage under Chronic Illness Benefit (when criteria met). Weight management not covered under chronic benefit; may be funded from day-to-day benefits.

## Saxenda (Liraglutide): The Registered Weight Loss Option

### What It Is
Saxenda is a daily injectable GLP-1 receptor agonist manufactured by Novo Nordisk. It contains the same active ingredient as Victoza (liraglutide) but at higher doses specifically approved for weight management.

### What SAHPRA Says
Saxenda is **the only GLP-1 agonist registered for chronic weight management in South Africa.** This is its key distinction from Ozempic and Mounjaro.

### Pricing in South Africa
- **Monthly maintenance (3 mg daily):** R4,500–R5,000

### Clinical Evidence for Weight Loss
The **SCALE trial** demonstrated:
- 56 weeks of treatment
- **Average weight loss: 8–10%** (less than semaglutide or tirzepatide)
- The lower efficacy is offset by its legal status as the only registered weight-loss GLP-1 in SA

### Why It's Being Phased Out
Novo Nordisk is reducing production of liraglutide products (Victoza and Saxenda) globally to increase manufacturing capacity for semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy). In South Africa, Victoza is still available, but Novo Nordisk has indicated they are "constantly evaluating our portfolio" and will notify the Ministry of Health before discontinuation.

This creates a supply risk for Saxenda. Patients on Saxenda should discuss alternative options with their physicians before supply constraints occur.

### Medical Aid Coverage
- **Discovery Health:** Funded from day-to-day benefits (medical savings account and above threshold), subject to plan rules.
- **Momentum:** Similar day-to-day coverage.
- **Other schemes:** Vary by plan.

## The Emerging Options: Compounded and New Molecules

### Compounded Semaglutide and Tirzepatide
Licensed compounding pharmacies in South Africa can prepare compounded versions of semaglutide and tirzepatide when:
- A physician determines it is clinically appropriate for a specific patient
- The commercial product is unavailable or unsuitable
- The patient has a valid prescription

**Pricing:** R1,500–R2,500/month (typically lower than brand-name Ozempic/Mounjaro)
**Important:** Compounded products are not SAHPRA-approved brand products. Quality, potency, and sterility depend on the specific compounding pharmacy. Always verify the pharmacy's SAPC registration and quality certifications.

### Retatrutide (Triple Agonist)
Retatrutide activates GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon receptors simultaneously. Phase 2 data showed **24% average weight loss** over 48 weeks — the highest ever recorded in a Phase 2 trial.

**Status in South Africa:** Not yet registered. Available only through specialized clinical research or compounding pathways with appropriate physician oversight.

### Wegovy and Rybelsus
- **Wegovy:** Semaglutide specifically approved for weight management in the US and EU. **Not yet registered in South Africa.** Novo Nordisk has not announced launch timing.
- **Rybelsus:** Oral semaglutide tablet. Registered by SAHPRA in 2024 but **not yet available** in the market. Novo Nordisk has not announced launch timing.

## How to Choose: Decision Framework

### If You Have Type 2 Diabetes
**First-line:** Ozempic or Mounjaro (both covered under Chronic Illness Benefit by major medical aids when criteria are met). Mounjaro has superior glycemic control and weight loss data. Discuss with your endocrinologist.

### If You Want Weight Loss (No Diabetes)
**Option 1: Saxenda** — The only SAHPRA-registered option for weight management. Higher cost, daily injection, lower efficacy than newer options. Risk of supply discontinuation.
**Option 2: Ozempic (off-label)** — Requires physician discretion. Weekly injection. Strong evidence. Not registered for weight loss, so medical aid coverage is from day-to-day only.
**Option 3: Mounjaro (off-label)** — Requires physician discretion. Weekly injection. Best clinical data. Weight-loss indication pending SAHPRA approval.
**Option 4: Compounded (semaglutide or tirzepatide)** — Lower cost. Requires physician prescription and licensed compounding pharmacy. Quality varies by pharmacy.

### If Cost Is a Primary Concern
Compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide through a legitimate telehealth platform is typically the most affordable option (R1,500–R2,500/month vs. R2,700–R4,600 for brand-name). However, ensure the compounding pharmacy is licensed and the product is quality-tested.

### If You Want the Best Clinical Data
Mounjaro (tirzepatide) has the strongest evidence with 20.9% average weight loss and superior performance in head-to-head trials against semaglutide.

### If You Need Medical Aid Coverage
- **T2D:** Ozempic or Mounjaro (Chronic Illness Benefit)
- **Weight loss only:** Saxenda (day-to-day benefits) or out-of-pocket for off-label options

## Safety Checklist: Before Starting Any GLP-1

### Must-Screen Contraindications
- [ ] Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC)
- [ ] Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN2)
- [ ] History of pancreatitis
- [ ] Pregnancy or breastfeeding
- [ ] Severe gastrointestinal disease (gastroparesis, Crohn's disease flares)
- [ ] Active eating disorder (anorexia, bulimia)
- [ ] Age under 18 (not approved for pediatric use)

### Must-Review Medications
- [ ] Insulin or sulfonylureas (hypoglycemia risk)
- [ ] Warfarin or other anticoagulants
- [ ] Oral medications (GLP-1s delay gastric emptying, affecting absorption)

### Must-Obtain Baseline Tests
- [ ] Fasting glucose and HbA1c
- [ ] Lipid panel
- [ ] Liver and kidney function
- [ ] Thyroid panel (TSH)
- [ ] Lipase and amylase (pancreatic enzymes)
- [ ] Pregnancy test (women of childbearing potential)

### Must-Commit To
- [ ] Weekly injection schedule (or daily for Saxenda)
- [ ] Monthly physician follow-ups for first 3 months
- [ ] Quarterly bloodwork
- [ ] Hydration protocol (minimum 2–3 liters/day)
- [ ] Protein target (1.2–1.6 g/kg body weight to prevent muscle loss)
- [ ] Resistance training (2–3× weekly to preserve lean mass)
- [ ] Understanding that stopping without lifestyle changes leads to weight regain

## Frequently Asked Questions (South Africa Specific)

### Why is Ozempic not registered for weight loss in South Africa?
Ozempic is registered for type 2 diabetes. Novo Nordisk's weight-loss brand (Wegovy) is the same compound at a higher dose but has not been launched in South Africa. SAHPRA registration requires separate clinical trial data and regulatory submissions for each indication. Novo Nordisk has not announced a launch date for Wegovy in SA.

### Can my GP prescribe Ozempic for weight loss?
Yes, legally, through off-label prescribing. However, not all GPs are comfortable with off-label GLP-1 prescribing, and some may lack experience with peptide therapy. Telehealth platforms specializing in metabolic medicine may offer more expertise.

### Is Mounjaro better than Ozempic?
Clinical evidence suggests yes — for both weight loss and glycemic control. The SURMOUNT-5 trial showed 6.5 percentage points more weight loss with tirzepatide than semaglutide. However, tirzepatide has slightly higher GI side effects. Individual response varies.

### Will medical aid cover Mounjaro for weight loss?
Not under Chronic Illness Benefit (unless you have T2D). May be covered from day-to-day benefits depending on your plan. Contact your medical aid directly. We provide itemized invoices for reimbursement claims.

### What if Saxenda is discontinued in South Africa?
If Novo Nordisk discontinues Saxenda, patients will need to transition to alternative options. Discuss this risk with your physician. Compounded liraglutide or transitioning to semaglutide/tirzepatide may be options.

### Are compounded GLP-1s safe?
Safety depends on the compounding pharmacy. Licensed SA pharmacies with proper quality control produce safe, effective compounds. Unregulated compounding or imported products carry risks. Always verify the pharmacy's SAPC registration and request COAs.

### How do I know if I am getting real semaglutide?
Brand-name Ozempic and Mounjaro have specific packaging, holograms, and batch numbers. Verify through the manufacturer's anti-counterfeit system. Compounded products should come with a Certificate of Analysis from the pharmacy. If the price is dramatically below market (e.g., R500/month for "semaglutide"), it is likely counterfeit or adulterated.

### Can I buy Ozempic from a pharmacy without a prescription?
No. Ozempic, Mounjaro, and Saxenda are Schedule 4 medicines. They require a prescription from an HPCSA-registered medical doctor. Any pharmacy selling them without a prescription is operating illegally.

### What about the "research peptide" websites?
As of 2026, SAHPRA has named multiple "research peptide" websites on its warning list for selling unauthorized and falsified GLP-1 products. The "research only" disclaimer is not a legal defense. These products are unregulated, potentially dangerous, and their sale is a criminal offense.

### How long should I stay on a GLP-1?
For weight loss, clinical trials used 68–72 weeks for active loss, followed by maintenance. Many patients benefit from ongoing therapy to prevent regain. For T2D, GLP-1s are typically long-term medications. Discuss a timeline with your physician based on your goals and response.

## The Bottom Line

South Africa's GLP-1 landscape in 2026 is characterized by:
- **High demand:** Obesity and diabetes epidemics create urgent need
- **Regulatory gaps:** Weight-loss indications are pending or absent for the most effective drugs
- **Supply constraints:** Saxenda may be discontinued; Wegovy and Rybelsus are not yet available
- **Enforcement:** SAHPRA is cracking down on unauthorized sellers, creating a trust vacuum
- **Emerging solutions:** Telehealth platforms with licensed physicians and compounding pharmacies are filling the gap

**The best option for most South African patients seeking weight loss:**
1. **Mounjaro (tirzepatide)** — best clinical data, weekly injection, pending weight-loss registration
2. **Ozempic (semaglutide)** — excellent data, weekly injection, available now, off-label for weight loss
3. **Compounded options** — most affordable, require careful pharmacy verification
4. **Saxenda** — only registered weight-loss option, but daily injection, higher cost, and supply risk

**The critical rule:** Never self-source GLP-1s from unregulated websites. Always work with an HPCSA-registered physician and a licensed pharmacy. The SAHPRA enforcement actions of 2026 have made this not just a health recommendation, but a legal necessity.
