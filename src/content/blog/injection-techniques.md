## Introduction

**Medical Disclaimer:** This content is for research and educational purposes only. It is not medical advice, nor a substitute for professional medical guidance. The techniques described below are intended for researchers working with peptide compounds in controlled settings. Peptides discussed here are not approved by SAHPRA or the FDA for human use. Always consult a qualified healthcare professional before administering any compound. This guide is harm-reduction content — we assume people will research regardless, so we provide the safest possible methodology.

Getting the injection technique right is as important as getting the dose right. A poorly administered injection can cause infection, abscess formation, nerve damage, or simply waste your compound through leakage or improper absorption. Conversely, a clean, well-executed injection is nearly painless, minimizes tissue trauma, and ensures your peptide reaches systemic circulation efficiently.

This guide covers every route of administration relevant to peptide research: subcutaneous (subQ), intramuscular (IM), intranasal, and oral. We include South African pharmacy sourcing, sharps disposal regulations, and step-by-step protocols you can follow precisely.

## Route Comparison: Which Administration Method?

Not all peptides behave the same across routes. Bioavailability, onset speed, and practical ease vary significantly. Choose your route based on the peptide's properties, your research goals, and your comfort level with sterile technique.

| Route | Bioavailability | Onset | Ease | Pain Level | Best For |
|-------|----------------|-------|------|-----------|----------|
| **Subcutaneous (subQ)** | 80–95% | 15–30 min | High | Very low (1/10) | Most peptides: BPC-157, TB-500, CJC-1295, Ipamorelin, Semaglutide |
| **Intramuscular (IM)** | 90–100% | 10–20 min | Moderate | Low (2/10) | Larger volumes, faster absorption needs, peptides like TB-500 |
| **Intranasal** | 30–70%* | 5–15 min | High | None | Selank, Semax, some nootropic peptides |
| **Oral (swallowed)** | 5–20%** | 30–60 min | Very high | None | BPC-157 (oral form), Semaglutide tablets |
| **Oral (sublingual)** | 15–35%** | 20–40 min | Moderate | None | BPC-157, limited evidence for others |

> *Intranasal bioavailability is highly variable depending on peptide molecular weight, spray droplet size, and mucosal condition. **Oral bioavailability varies enormously by peptide; most are destroyed by gastric acid and first-pass metabolism.

**Key Takeaway:** Subcutaneous injection is the gold standard for peptide research. It offers excellent bioavailability with minimal pain, requires the smallest needles, and carries the lowest risk profile. Intramuscular is appropriate when you need the fastest possible onset or are injecting larger volumes. Intranasal and oral are convenience options for specific peptides where formulation data supports their use.

## Needle and Syringe Selection Guide

The right equipment makes every difference in comfort, safety, and dosing accuracy. For peptide research, you will almost exclusively use insulin syringes — they are designed for small volumes, have ultra-fine needles, and are marked in units that make micro-dosing straightforward.

### Understanding Gauge and Length

| Gauge | Needle Length | Typical Use | Pain Level | Notes |
|-------|--------------|-------------|-----------|-------|
| **31G** | 6 mm | Subcutaneous | Minimal | Thinnest available; ideal for abdominal fat |
| **30G** | 8 mm | Subcutaneous | Very low | Slightly sturdier; good for thigh or upper arm |
| **29G** | 12 mm | Intramuscular | Low | Short IM needle; sufficient for deltoid and thigh |
| **27G** | 13 mm | Intramuscular | Low-moderate | Standard IM; rarely needed for peptides |
| **25G** | 16 mm | Intramuscular | Moderate | Overkill for most peptide research |

**Gauge explained:** The higher the number, the thinner the needle. 31G is approximately 0.25 mm in diameter. Thinner needles cause less tissue trauma and less pain. For peptide research, there is rarely any reason to go below 29G.

### Syringe Volume

| Volume | Best For | Notes |
|--------|----------|-------|
| **0.3 mL (30 units)** | Very small doses (<200 mcg) | Less dead space, very precise |
| **0.5 mL (50 units)** | Small to moderate doses | Good balance of precision and capacity |
| **1.0 mL (100 units)** | Moderate to larger doses | Most versatile; standard for peptide research |
| **3.0 mL** | Large volume reconstitution | Not for injection; use for mixing only |

**Recommendation:** Keep 1 mL U-100 insulin syringes as your default. They have 100 unit markings, where 100 units = 1 mL. This makes dose calculation simple if you know your concentration in mg/mL.

### Where to Buy in South Africa

| Item | Where to Buy | Prescription Required? | Approximate Price |
|------|-------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| 31G 6mm insulin syringes (0.3–1 mL) | Clicks, Dis-Chem, independent pharmacies | No | R3–6 per syringe |
| 30G 8mm insulin syringes | Clicks, Dis-Chem, independent pharmacies | No | R3–5 per syringe |
| 29G 12mm insulin syringes | Clicks, Dis-Chem, medical supply stores | No | R4–7 per syringe |
| Alcohol swabs (70% isopropyl) | Any pharmacy | No | R25–40 per box of 100 |
| Sharps containers | Pharmacies, clinics, medical supply stores | No | R40–80 |
| Bacteriostatic water | Compounding pharmacies | Sometimes | R80–150 per 10 mL |

**Important:** In South Africa, insulin syringes up to 1 mL are available without prescription under the Pharmacy Act. You do not need to be diabetic to purchase them. If a pharmacist refuses, ask for "diabetic needles for insulin" or visit a different pharmacy. Dis-Chem and Clicks stock these routinely. For larger quantities, medical supply stores like Medisupply or local equivalents often sell boxes of 100 at bulk pricing.

## Step-by-Step Subcutaneous Injection Technique

Subcutaneous injections deposit the peptide into the fatty tissue just beneath the skin. This layer has excellent blood supply for absorption, few nerve endings (reducing pain), and is easy to access. It is the preferred route for nearly all peptide research.

### Before You Begin

1. **Wash your hands** thoroughly with soap and warm water for at least 20 seconds
2. **Gather supplies:** reconstituted peptide vial, alcohol swabs, insulin syringe, sharps container
3. **Check the peptide:** Clear solution, no particles, no cloudiness (unless expected for that peptide), within expiry date
4. **Let the vial come to room temperature** if refrigerated — cold injections sting more

### Step 1: Prepare the Syringe

1. Wipe the peptide vial's rubber stopper with an alcohol swab. Let it dry for 10 seconds.
2. Remove the syringe cap. Do not touch the needle.
3. Draw air into the syringe equal to your intended dose volume.
4. Insert the needle through the vial stopper.
5. Inject the air into the vial (this prevents vacuum from making withdrawal difficult).
6. Invert the vial. Keep the needle tip submerged in the liquid.
7. Withdraw your dose slowly. Tap the syringe barrel to move any air bubbles to the top.
8. Push out excess air until a tiny droplet appears at the needle tip. You now have your exact dose.

### Step 2: Select and Prepare the Injection Site

**Recommended subQ sites for peptides:**

- **Abdominal fat:** 2 inches (5 cm) away from the navel, either side. This is the easiest site with the most consistent fat layer.
- **Thigh fat:** Outer or front of the thigh, at least 4 inches above the knee and 4 inches below the hip.
- **Upper arm fat:** Back of the upper arm, in the fatty area. Requires assistance or flexibility.
- **Lower back/love handles:** Flank fat, accessible when sitting.

1. Choose a site at least 1 inch away from your last injection.
2. Wipe the site with an alcohol swab in a circular motion, moving outward from the center.
3. Let the alcohol dry completely — injecting through wet alcohol causes a sharp sting.

### Step 3: Perform the Injection

1. **Pinch the skin** firmly between your thumb and index finger to create a raised fat pad. This lifts the subcutaneous layer away from underlying muscle.
2. **Insert the needle** at a 90-degree angle (perpendicular) to the skin surface. With 6–8 mm needles and adequate fat, this ensures subcutaneous placement. If you are very lean, a 45-degree angle may be safer.
3. **Release the pinch** once the needle is fully inserted.
4. **Depress the plunger slowly** — take 5–10 seconds to inject the full volume. Rapid injection causes tissue distension and pain.
5. **Hold for 5 seconds** after the plunger is fully depressed. This allows the liquid to disperse and prevents backflow when you withdraw.
6. **Withdraw the needle** smoothly at the same angle it entered.
7. **Apply gentle pressure** with a clean cotton swab or gauze if there is minor bleeding. Do not rub.

### Step 4: Post-Injection

1. **Dispose of the needle immediately** in a sharps container. Never recap a used needle.
2. **Note the site, time, dose, and peptide** in your research log.
3. **Monitor the site** for 24 hours. Mild redness or a tiny bump is normal. Increasing pain, heat, swelling, or pus indicates infection — seek medical attention.

## Step-by-Step Intramuscular Injection Technique

Intramuscular injections deposit peptide directly into muscle tissue, where blood flow is higher and absorption is faster. For most peptides, this offers no meaningful advantage over subQ, but some researchers prefer IM for larger doses or specific protocols.

### Suitable IM Sites for Self-Administration

| Site | Muscle | Needle Length | Accessibility | Risk Level |
|------|--------|--------------|-------------|------------|
| **Deltoid** | Lateral shoulder | 12–16 mm | Easy (self) | Low |
| **Vastus lateralis** | Outer thigh | 12–16 mm | Easy (self) | Very low |
| **Ventrogluteal** | Hip/glute | 16–25 mm | Difficult (self) | Low |

**Recommendation:** For peptide research, the deltoid and vastus lateralis are sufficient. The ventrogluteal site is excellent but difficult to reach yourself and carries a small risk of sciatic nerve contact if misplaced.

### Deltoid IM Injection

1. **Locate the site:** On the lateral (outer) aspect of the upper arm, approximately 2–3 inches below the acromion (bony tip of the shoulder) and 1–2 inches above the armpit line. The muscle belly should feel firm when tensed.
2. **Cleanse** with an alcohol swab and let dry.
3. **Relax the arm** — let it hang loosely or support it on a surface. Tensed muscle increases resistance and pain.
4. **Insert the needle** at 90 degrees, straight into the muscle belly, with a quick, dart-like motion. Hesitation causes more pain.
5. **Aspirate:** Pull back slightly on the plunger. If you see blood entering the syringe, you have hit a blood vessel. Withdraw, discard the needle and dose, and start fresh with a new syringe at a different site.
6. **If no blood appears, inject slowly** over 5–10 seconds.
7. **Hold for 5 seconds, withdraw smoothly**, and apply gentle pressure with gauze.

### Vastus Lateralis IM Injection

1. **Locate the site:** On the outer (lateral) aspect of the thigh. Divide your thigh into thirds vertically — the injection site is the middle third, on the outer side.
2. **Sit or stand** with the leg relaxed.
3. **Cleanse, insert at 90 degrees, aspirate, inject, hold, withdraw** — same protocol as deltoid.

### IM-Specific Safety Notes

- **Never inject into a muscle that is sore, bruised, or infected.**
- **Rotate sites aggressively** — IM tissue is less forgiving than fat. Give each muscle at least 7 days to recover.
- **Do not inject more than 2 mL** into the deltoid or 3 mL into the vastus lateralis. Peptide doses are typically well below this.
- **If you hit bone** (possible in very lean individuals with short needles), withdraw slightly and redirect.

## Intranasal Administration Guide

Intranasal delivery bypasses the gastrointestinal tract and first-pass liver metabolism by absorbing through the nasal mucosa directly into systemic circulation. It is non-invasive, painless, and convenient. However, bioavailability is unpredictable and peptide-dependent.

### Peptides Suitable for Intranasal Use

| Peptide | Intranasal Viability | Notes |
|---------|---------------------|-------|
| **Selank** | High | Developed specifically for intranasal delivery |
| **Semax** | High | Developed specifically for intranasal delivery |
| **BPC-157** | Moderate | Some researchers report efficacy; limited data |
| **CJC-1295** | Low | Large molecule; poor nasal absorption |
| **TB-500** | Low | Large molecule; unlikely to absorb well |
| **Ipamorelin** | Low | Not formulated for nasal delivery |

### Equipment

- **Nasal spray bottle** with metered dose pump (typically 0.1 mL per spray)
- **Peptide solution** reconstituted in bacteriostatic water
- **Optional:** Nasal absorption enhancer (e.g., dimethyl-β-cyclodextrin) — research-grade only

### Step-by-Step Intranasal Protocol

1. **Clear your nasal passages.** Blow your nose gently. A clear passage maximizes mucosal contact.
2. **Prime the spray bottle** if new. Pump until a fine mist is produced.
3. **Load your peptide solution** into the nasal spray bottle using a sterile syringe.
4. **Tilt your head slightly forward** (not back — this causes runoff down the throat).
5. **Insert the nozzle** just into the nostril, aiming slightly outward toward the ear (away from the nasal septum).
6. **Spray while inhaling gently.** Do not snort forcefully — this deposits peptide in the throat, not the mucosa.
7. **Repeat in the other nostril** if your dose requires it.
8. **Do not blow your nose** for at least 10 minutes post-administration.

### Maximizing Intranasal Absorption

- **Administer when nasal mucosa is well-hydrated** — dry mucosa absorbs poorly.
- **Avoid immediately after nasal decongestant use** — vasoconstricted mucosa reduces absorption.
- **Consider a light saline rinse** 30 minutes before if you have allergies or congestion.
- **Divide doses** into both nostrils rather than one large spray.

## Site Rotation Strategy

Repeated injection into the same site causes lipohypertrophy (fatty lumps), scar tissue, reduced absorption, and increased pain. A systematic rotation strategy prevents all of these issues.

### Subcutaneous Rotation Map

Imagine your abdomen as a clock face with the navel at the center. You have 8 valid injection zones:

```
        12 o'clock (upper abdomen, midline)
              |
    10 o'clock     2 o'clock
         \           /
          \    o    /    <- navel (avoid 2-inch radius)
           \  ( )  /
    8 o'clock     4 o'clock
              |
        6 o'clock (lower abdomen)
```

**Rotation protocol:**
- Use one "time slot" per day, moving clockwise.
- After completing the abdominal cycle, switch to thigh sites (left outer, left front, right outer, right front).
- Then upper arms if accessible.
- Return to abdomen after a full cycle.
- **Minimum spacing:** 1 inch (2.5 cm) from any previous injection mark.

### IM Rotation Map

For IM, the cycle is simpler due to fewer suitable self-injection sites:

| Day | Site |
|-----|------|
| Monday | Left deltoid |
| Wednesday | Right vastus lateralis |
| Friday | Right deltoid |
| Next Monday | Left vastus lateralis |
| Next Wednesday | Left deltoid |

**Minimum recovery time:** 72 hours for the same muscle. 7 days is ideal.

### Tracking Your Rotation

Use a simple grid in your research notebook:

```
Date | Peptide | Dose | Site | Side | Notes
-----|---------|------|------|------|------
     |         |      |      |      |
```

Marking sites prevents accidental same-site injection and helps you identify which locations cause the least discomfort.

## Safety Protocols: Non-Negotiable Practices

Sterile technique is not optional. An infection from a sloppy injection can land you in hospital with an abscess, cellulitis, or worse. These protocols are the minimum standard.

### Sterile Technique Checklist

| Step | Action | Why It Matters |
|------|--------|---------------|
| 1 | Wash hands with soap and water for 20+ seconds | Removes transient bacteria |
| 2 | Clean work surface with 70% alcohol | Reduces environmental contamination |
| 3 | Use new needle and syringe for every injection | Prevents bacterial transfer and needle dullness |
| 4 | Wipe vial stopper with alcohol before every penetration | Stopper is not sterile after first use |
| 5 | Let alcohol dry before needle contact | Wet alcohol stings and may carry surface bacteria into tissue |
| 6 | Do not touch needle tip, shaft, or plunger tip | Touch = contamination |
| 7 | Do not recap used needles | Most needlestick injuries occur during recapping |
| 8 | Dispose of sharps immediately after use | Prevents accidental sticks and contamination |

### Sharps Disposal in South Africa

South Africa has specific regulations for medical waste disposal under the **National Health Act, 2003 (Act No. 61 of 2003)** and **SANS 10248** standards for healthcare waste management.

**For individual researchers:**

| Disposal Method | How | Notes |
|----------------|-----|-------|
| **Pharmacy return** | Return sealed sharps container to Clicks, Dis-Chem, or local pharmacy | Most pharmacies accept sealed sharps containers; call ahead to confirm |
| **Clinic drop-off** | Local government clinic or community health centre | Free; accepted during operating hours |
| **Private waste service** | Contact a medical waste contractor (e.g., Compass Medical Waste, Safsane) | Costly for small quantities; better for bulk |
| **Mail-back programs** | Some online medical suppliers offer prepaid return containers | Convenient but expensive |

**Critical rules:**
- **Never dispose of loose needles** in household rubbish.
- **Never flush needles** down toilets.
- **Use a proper sharps container** — rigid, puncture-resistant, sealable. If unavailable temporarily, a thick plastic detergent bottle with a screw cap is an emergency substitute (still return it to a pharmacy, do not bin it).
- **Label the container** "SHARPS — DO NOT OPEN" when full.

### Never Share Needles or Vials

This cannot be overstated. Sharing injection equipment transmits blood-borne pathogens including hepatitis B, hepatitis C, and HIV. Even sharing a reconstituted vial between two people using separate needles is risky — backflow into the vial during needle withdrawal contaminates the entire solution.

**If multiple subjects are part of your research:**
- Each subject gets their own vial, syringes, and alcohol swabs.
- Label everything with the subject identifier.
- Store separately.

## Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

| Mistake | Consequence | How to Avoid |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| **Reusing needles** | Dull needle causes tissue trauma, pain, and infection risk | New needle every time; they cost R3–6 |
| **Injecting through wet alcohol** | Chemical burn sensation, possible bacterial introduction | Wait 10 seconds after swabbing |
| **Forgetting to aspirate (IM)** | Intravascular injection can cause rapid, unexpected systemic effects | Always aspirate before depressing plunger |
| **Injecting too fast** | Tissue distension, pain, leakage back out | Take 5–10 seconds per injection |
| **Not rotating sites** | Lipohypertrophy, scar tissue, erratic absorption | Use a rotation log |
| **Shaking the peptide vial** | Foam and potential denaturation | Swirl gently; never shake |
| **Injecting cold peptide** | Increased pain from thermal shock | Let vial reach room temperature before drawing |
| **Using the wrong needle angle** | Intramuscular placement with subQ needle (painful) or subQ placement with IM intent (erratic absorption) | 90 degrees for subQ with adequate fat; 45 degrees if lean |
| **Leaving air bubbles** | Inaccurate dosing; minor risk with large bubbles | Tap syringe, push out air before injecting |
| **Not tracking doses** | Overdosing, underdosing, missed doses | Maintain a dated log |
| **Improper storage** | Degraded peptide, bacterial growth | Refrigerate at 2–8°C; do not freeze reconstituted |

## Pain Reduction Techniques

Most peptide injections should cause minimal discomfort — a brief pinch at most. If you are experiencing significant pain, something in your technique or equipment needs adjustment.

### Equipment Adjustments

| Change | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| Switch to 31G 6mm needles | Thinnest needle available; dramatically reduces pain |
| Use 0.3 mL syringes for small doses | Smaller barrel = less pressure needed = smoother injection |
| Ensure needles are truly sharp | Dull needles (damaged or reused) tear tissue instead of cutting |
| Room-temperature peptide | Cold liquid stings; 5 minutes out of the fridge is sufficient |

### Technique Adjustments

1. **Relax the target area.** Tensed muscle or pinched fat increases resistance and pain. Breathe out as you insert the needle.
2. **Insert quickly, withdraw slowly.** A swift, confident insertion minimizes nerve stimulation. Withdrawal should be smooth and deliberate.
3. **Inject slowly.** Rapid plunger depression distends tissue and activates stretch receptors. 5–10 seconds per 0.5 mL is appropriate.
4. **Do not change needle angle during insertion.** A wobbling needle tears more tissue than a straight path.
5. **Apply gentle pressure after, do not rub.** Rubbing irritates the site and can spread any surface bacteria.

### Post-Injection Care

- **Warm compress** 10 minutes post-injection can reduce any local irritation.
- **Avoid strenuous exercise** of the injected muscle for 2–4 hours (IM only).
- **If a bruise forms,** note it and avoid that exact spot for 2 weeks.

## South African Supply Considerations

### Pharmacy Availability

South Africa's pharmacy sector is well-developed, and the supplies needed for peptide research are generally accessible without prescription:

| Supply | Availability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| Insulin syringes (all gauges listed) | Excellent | Clicks, Dis-Chem, independent pharmacies; no prescription |
| Alcohol swabs | Excellent | Every pharmacy stocks these |
| Sharps containers | Good | Most pharmacies; sometimes kept behind counter — ask |
| Bacteriostatic water | Moderate | Compounding pharmacies (e.g., Pharma Dynamics, Fagron); some require script |
| Sterile water for injection | Good | More widely available than BAC water; acceptable for single-use reconstitution |
| Gauze and adhesive strips | Excellent | Any pharmacy |

### Regional Variations

- **Major metros (JHB, CPT, DBN):** Full availability at chain pharmacies.
- **Smaller towns:** Independent pharmacies may need to order syringes in bulk. Call ahead.
- **Rural areas:** Clinic visits may be necessary for sharps disposal; syringe availability varies.

### Cost-Saving Tips

- **Buy syringes in boxes of 100** from medical supply stores rather than individual packs at retail pharmacies. Cost drops to R1.50–2.50 per syringe.
- **Sharps containers** can be reused until full if sealed properly between uses. A 5L container holds approximately 200–300 syringes.
- **Alcohol swabs** are cheaper in boxes of 200 than small packs.

### Regulatory Context

Peptides themselves exist in a regulatory grey area in South Africa. They are not scheduled substances under the Medicines and Related Substances Act, but they are not approved by SAHPRA for human use. The injection supplies (syringes, BAC water) are fully legal and unrestricted. Your research documentation and sterile technique are your primary protections.
