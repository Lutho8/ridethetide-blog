## What Are Peptides?

Peptides are short chains of amino acids — typically 2 to 50 amino acids linked by peptide bonds. Unlike full proteins, peptides are small enough to penetrate cell membranes and interact with specific receptors, making them highly targeted signaling molecules.

Your body produces thousands of peptides naturally. They regulate growth hormone release, insulin secretion, immune response, tissue repair, and cellular metabolism. Therapeutic peptides are synthetic versions designed to amplify or restore these natural functions.

In South Africa, peptides have emerged from a niche bodybuilding subculture into mainstream health conversations. The driving forces are:
- **The obesity epidemic:** 27% of South African adults are obese. Weight-related diseases have overtaken TB and HIV as leading causes of mortality.
- **The GLP-1 revolution:** Semaglutide, tirzepatide, and retatrutide have demonstrated unprecedented weight loss results in clinical trials.
- **The aging population:** South Africans are living longer and seeking interventions for energy, recovery, and cellular health.
- **The sports performance market:** Competitive and recreational athletes are exploring recovery and optimization tools.

## How Peptides Work: The Mechanisms

### 1. Growth Hormone Axis Peptides
These peptides stimulate your pituitary gland to release growth hormone (GH) in a pulsatile pattern.

**Examples:** CJC-1295, Ipamorelin, Sermorelin, Hexarelin, GHRP-6, GHRP-2

**Mechanism:**
- CJC-1295 is a GHRH analog that stimulates GH release via GHRH receptors.
- Ipamorelin is a ghrelin receptor agonist that drives GH release through a separate pathway.
- When combined, they produce synergistic GH pulses greater than either alone.

**Effects:**
- Increased lean muscle mass
- Reduced visceral fat (via hormone-sensitive lipase activation)
- Improved sleep quality and recovery
- Enhanced skin elasticity and collagen synthesis
- Better bone density

### 2. GLP-1 Receptor Agonists (The Metabolic Revolution)
GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) is a hormone your gut releases after eating. It signals satiety, stimulates insulin secretion, and slows gastric emptying. Therapeutic GLP-1 agonists are modified to last days instead of minutes.

**Examples:** Semaglutide, Tirzepatide, Retatrutide, Liraglutide

**Mechanism:**
- **Semaglutide** activates GLP-1 receptors in the brain (hypothalamus) to reduce appetite, in the pancreas to improve insulin secretion, and in the stomach to slow gastric emptying.
- **Tirzepatide** activates both GLP-1 and GIP receptors. GIP enhances the GLP-1 effect and may have independent effects on adipose tissue.
- **Retatrutide** activates GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon receptors. The glucagon receptor activation increases energy expenditure directly.

**Evidence:**
- **Semaglutide (STEP-1):** 1,961 participants, 68 weeks. Average weight loss: 14.9% (vs 2.4% placebo). (Wilding et al., NEJM 2021)
- **Tirzepatide (SURMOUNT-1):** 2,539 participants, 72 weeks. Average weight loss: 20.9% at 15 mg dose. (Jastreboff et al., NEJM 2022)
- **Tirzepatide vs. Semaglutide (SURMOUNT-5):** Tirzepatide 20.2% vs. Semaglutide 13.7%. (2024)
- **Retatrutide (Phase 2):** 24% average weight reduction over 48 weeks at highest dose. (Eli Lilly, NEJM 2023)

### 3. Tissue Repair and Healing Peptides
These peptides accelerate tissue regeneration, reduce inflammation, and promote angiogenesis.

**Examples:** BPC-157, TB-500 (Thymosin Beta-4), GHK-Cu, LL-37

**Mechanism:**
- **BPC-157** modulates nitric oxide, growth factor expression, and collagen organization. It promotes healing in tendons, ligaments, muscles, and the gastrointestinal tract.
- **TB-500** regulates actin, promotes cell migration to wound sites, and reduces inflammatory cytokines.
- **GHK-Cu** stimulates collagen synthesis, angiogenesis, and tissue remodeling.

### 4. Mitochondrial and Metabolic Peptides
These peptides target cellular energy production, insulin sensitivity, and metabolic flexibility.

**Examples:** MOTS-C, AOD-9604, Tesamorelin

**Mechanism:**
- **MOTS-C** activates AMPK (AMP-activated protein kinase), increasing fatty acid oxidation and improving insulin sensitivity.
- **AOD-9604** stimulates lipolysis via beta-3 adrenergic receptors on adipocytes without systemic GH effects.
- **Tesamorelin** stimulates pituitary GH release and is FDA-approved for HIV-associated lipodystrophy.

### 5. Cellular Health and Longevity Peptides
These peptides target DNA repair, telomere maintenance, antioxidant defense, and cellular senescence.

**Examples:** NAD+, Epitalon, Glutathione, DSIP

**Mechanism:**
- **NAD+** is essential for cellular energy production, DNA repair (via PARP enzymes), and sirtuin activation. NAD+ levels decline by approximately 50% between age 20 and 60.
- **Epitalon** activates telomerase, the enzyme that extends telomeres.
- **Glutathione** is the body's master antioxidant, neutralizing reactive oxygen species.
- **DSIP** promotes deep, restorative sleep and modulates stress hormones.

## The South African Regulatory Landscape

### SAHPRA and the Medicines Act
The South African Health Products Regulatory Authority (SAHPRA) regulates all medicines under the **Medicines and Related Substances Act (Act 101 of 1965)**.

- **Section 29:** It is a criminal offense to sell a registerable medicine without SAHPRA registration.
- **Schedule 4:** Most therapeutic peptides (including GLP-1 agonists) are classified as prescription-only medicines.
- **Compounding:** Licensed compounding pharmacies can prepare customized medications for individual patients when a commercial product is unsuitable or unavailable.

### SAHPRA Enforcement in 2026
In 2024–2026, SAHPRA escalated enforcement against the "research peptide" gray market. Specific websites were named for selling unauthorized, falsified, or compounded GLP-1 products without registration. This includes researchpeptides.co.za, juiceheads.co.za, anabolicsza.com, and others.

**What this means for buyers:**
- Buying from SAHPRA-named vendors means purchasing unverified, potentially dangerous products.
- The "research purposes only" disclaimer provides no legal protection to the seller or safety assurance to the buyer.
- The only legal and safe pathway is through a physician-prescribed, pharmacy-compounded protocol.

### What Is Legal?
✅ **Legal:**
- Consulting an HPCSA-registered physician about peptide therapy
- Obtaining a prescription for a peptide protocol
- Having the prescription filled by a SAPC-registered compounding pharmacy
- Using peptides under physician supervision with regular monitoring
- Importing personal-use medications with a valid prescription (subject to customs regulations)

❌ **Illegal:**
- Selling peptides online without SAHPRA registration
- Selling peptides without requiring a prescription
- Marketing peptides for human use while claiming they are "for research only"
- Importing unregistered medicines for commercial resale
- Compounding peptides in unregistered facilities

## Safety, Side Effects, and Contraindications

### Common Side Effects by Peptide Class

**GLP-1 Agonists (Semaglutide, Tirzepatide):**
- Nausea (30–50% in first 4 weeks)
- Diarrhea (15–25%)
- Vomiting (10–20%)
- Constipation (10–15%)
- Abdominal pain (5–10%)
- Fatigue (5–10%)
- **Management:** Start low, go slow. Eat smaller meals. Avoid high-fat foods. Stay hydrated. Side effects typically resolve within 2–4 weeks.
- **Serious (rare):** Pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, severe dehydration. Seek emergency care for intense abdominal pain.

**GH Secretagogues (CJC-1295, Ipamorelin, Sermorelin):**
- Water retention (mild, transient)
- Increased hunger (Ipamorelin less than GHRP-6)
- Numbness/tingling at injection site
- Mild fatigue (transient)
- **Caution:** Can raise IGF-1 and blood glucose. Monitor in diabetics and those with insulin resistance.

**Healing Peptides (BPC-157, TB-500):**
- Generally well-tolerated
- Mild injection site reactions
- Rare: flu-like symptoms (transient)
- **Caution:** Theoretical concern about promoting growth in undiagnosed cancers. Physician screening required.

**Metabolic Peptides (MOTS-C, AOD-9604):**
- Minimal side effects reported in research
- MOTS-C: mild fatigue or hypoglycemia symptoms in some users
- AOD-9604: generally well-tolerated

### Major Contraindications

**Absolute Contraindications (Do NOT use without explicit physician approval):**
- Personal or family history of **medullary thyroid carcinoma** (GLP-1 black box warning)
- **Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2** (MEN2)
- Active **pancreatitis** or history of pancreatitis
- **Pregnancy** or breastfeeding (all peptide classes)
- Active **eating disorder** (anorexia, bulimia)
- Severe **gastrointestinal disease** (gastroparesis, inflammatory bowel disease flares)
- **Diabetic retinopathy** with recent worsening (GH axis peptides)
- Active **cancer** or recent cancer treatment (GH axis peptides)

**Relative Contraindications (Require careful monitoring):**
- Type 1 diabetes (GLP-1 agonists)
- Multiple diabetes medications (hypoglycemia risk)
- Kidney disease (dose adjustments needed)
- Liver disease (metabolism concerns)
- History of depression or suicidal ideation
- Gallbladder disease
- Heart failure
- Age > 75 (reduced dosing, closer monitoring)

### Drug Interactions

**GLP-1 Agonists:**
- **Sulfonylureas and insulin:** Hypoglycemia risk. Dose reductions may be needed.
- **Warfarin:** Possible interaction; monitor INR.
- **Oral medications:** Delayed gastric emptying may affect absorption of oral drugs. Take oral meds 1 hour before GLP-1 injection.

**GH Secretagogues:**
- **Corticosteroids:** May blunt GH response.
- **Thyroid medications:** Monitor thyroid function; GH affects thyroid hormone conversion.

**Healing Peptides:**
- Few known drug interactions. Always disclose all medications to your physician.

## How to Use Peptides: Practical Administration

### Reconstitution
Most peptides are shipped as lyophilized (freeze-dried) powder in vials. They must be reconstituted with bacteriostatic water.

**Steps:**
1. Wash hands thoroughly. Clean the vial top with an alcohol swab.
2. Draw bacteriostatic water into a syringe (typically 1–2 ml per vial).
3. Inject the water slowly down the side of the vial (not directly onto the powder).
4. Gently swirl until dissolved. Do not shake vigorously.
5. Store reconstituted peptides in the refrigerator (2–8°C) for up to 28 days.
6. Label the vial with the reconstitution date and concentration.

### Injection Techniques
**Subcutaneous (most common):**
- Inject into fatty tissue: abdomen (2 inches from navel), thigh, or upper arm.
- Pinch the skin, insert needle at 45–90 degrees, inject slowly, release pinch, withdraw.
- Rotate injection sites to prevent lipohypertrophy (fat lumps).

**Needle Size:**
- Subcutaneous: 29–31 gauge, ½ inch (12.7 mm) needle.
- Intramuscular: 25–27 gauge, 1 inch (25 mm) needle.

### Storage
- **Lyophilized (unreconstituted):** Store in the freezer (-20°C) for up to 24 months. Or in the refrigerator (2–8°C) for up to 12 months. Keep away from light.
- **Reconstituted:** Refrigerate (2–8°C) for up to 28 days. Do not freeze reconstituted peptides. Protect from light.
- **During travel:** Use a medical cooler bag with ice packs. Do not leave in a hot car.
- **Degradation signs:** Cloudiness, discoloration, particles, or loss of potency. Do not use if these appear.

### Cycling
Most peptide protocols benefit from cycling to prevent receptor desensitization and maintain effectiveness.

**Common Cycles:**
- **GH Secretagogues:** 12 weeks on, 4 weeks off.
- **GLP-1 Agonists:** Continuous use for weight loss (clinical trials used 68–72 weeks). Taper for maintenance.
- **Healing Peptides:** 4–6 weeks on for acute injury; 8–12 weeks for chronic issues. Maintenance as needed.
- **Longevity Peptides:** Continuous low-dose or pulsed cycles (e.g., Epitalon: 10-day cycles twice yearly).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are peptides legal in South Africa?
Yes, when prescribed by an HPCSA-registered physician and dispensed by a licensed pharmacy. It is illegal to sell peptides for human use without SAHPRA registration or to sell them without a prescription. The "research only" sale model is a legal fiction and violates Section 29 of the Medicines Act.

### Do peptides work?
The evidence varies by peptide. GLP-1 agonists (semaglutide, tirzepatide) have the strongest evidence with large Phase 3 trials showing 15–22% weight loss. GH secretagogues have moderate evidence for GH elevation and body recomposition. Healing peptides have promising animal data but limited human RCTs. Longevity peptides are largely preclinical. Always discuss evidence with your physician.

### How long before I see results?
- GLP-1 weight loss: 4–8 weeks for visible results, 6–12 months for peak results.
- GH secretagogues: 4–6 weeks for sleep/recovery, 8–12 weeks for body composition.
- Healing peptides: 2–4 weeks for acute injuries, 6–12 weeks for chronic issues.
- Longevity peptides: 2–6 weeks for subjective energy/sleep, 3–6 months for biomarker changes.

### Will I regain weight if I stop?
Yes, if you stop GLP-1 therapy without maintaining lifestyle changes. Clinical trials show weight regain within weeks of discontinuation. These are obesity treatments, not cures. Long-term maintenance requires either ongoing therapy or permanent lifestyle modification.

### Can I use peptides with other supplements?
Most supplements (vitamins, minerals, omega-3s, creatine) are safe with peptides. However, some herbal supplements affect blood sugar or clotting and may interact. Always disclose all supplements to your physician.

### Are peptides steroids?
No. Peptides are short chains of amino acids. Some peptides (like GH secretagogues) have anabolic effects, but they work through entirely different mechanisms than anabolic steroids. They do not suppress natural testosterone production or cause the same side effect profiles.

### What is the best peptide for beginners?
For weight loss, semaglutide is the most accessible and well-studied starting point. For recovery, BPC-157 has the most user-friendly profile. For longevity, NAD+ or Sermorelin are common entry points. The "best" peptide depends on your goals, health profile, and physician assessment.

### How do I know if a peptide supplier is legitimate?
See the 10-point verification checklist above. If they don't require a prescription, don't have a physician, don't have a licensed pharmacy, or are on SAHPRA's warning list — they are not legitimate.

### Can I travel with peptides?
Yes, with a valid prescription and documentation. Keep peptides refrigerated during travel. Carry a copy of your prescription and a letter from your physician. Declare at customs if asked. Do not check refrigerated medications in luggage for long flights without a medical cooler.
