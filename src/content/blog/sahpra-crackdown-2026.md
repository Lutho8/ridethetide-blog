The South African Health Products Regulatory Authority (SAHPRA) has moved from caution to enforcement. In 2024–2026, SAHPRA escalated against the "research peptide" gray market, naming specific websites selling unauthorized, falsified, or compounded GLP-1 products.

If you have bought peptides online in South Africa — or are considering it — this guide covers what SAHPRA has actually done, which websites are named, what "research purposes only" means under South African law, and how to access peptides safely through a legitimate clinical platform.

## What SAHPRA Announced

On 8 November 2024, SAHPRA issued a public statement expressing concern over falsified, compounded, and substandard GLP-1 products sold through websites and informal channels. By 2026, this concern became enforcement action.

SAHPRA named specific websites selling unauthorized versions of semaglutide and tirzepatide:

| Website | Named Products |
|---------|---------------|
| juiceheads.co.za | Body Pharm Semaglutide, HD Labs Semaglutide, HD Labs Tirzepatide, Body Pharm Tirzepatide |
| anabolicsza.com | Semaglutide vials (EU Pharmaceuticals), Platinum Tirzepatide, Lira Tirzepatide, Novo-lela Tirzepatide, and 10+ others |
| sallyslimming.co.za | Novaglutide |
| slimnburn.co.za | Novazepatide |
| **researchpeptides.co.za** | **AAP (Anti Appetite Peptide)** |

Research Peptides South Africa — the country's largest "research peptide" supplier — is now on SAHPRA's enforcement list. The authority stated that this list is **not exhaustive** and urged the public to report additional sellers.

### The Legal Implications

SAHPRA was explicit: "The sale of a registerable medicine without registration is an offence in terms of Section 29 of the Medicines and Related Substances Act. Those who commit an offence... shall be held criminally liable and applicable penalties shall be enforced."

This means:
- **Sellers** face criminal prosecution for selling unregistered medicines
- **Buyers** are purchasing products with no verified safety, efficacy, or quality data
- **Healthcare professionals** who prescribe or recommend these products risk HPCSA disciplinary action

Dr. Boitumelo Semete-Makokotlela, SAHPRA CEO, stressed that the authority is actively monitoring supply chains and online platforms for unregistered, substandard, and falsified medicines.

## What "Research Purposes Only" Actually Means

The gray-market peptide industry in South Africa has operated on a legal fiction: selling peptides "for research purposes only" with a disclaimer that they are "not for human consumption."

### The Gray Market Model
1. Import raw peptide powder from unregulated overseas labs
2. Repackage into vials with generic labels
3. Sell online with a "research only" disclaimer
4. Provide no medical oversight, no prescription, no physician consultation, no batch testing verification
5. Accept payment via EFT, cryptocurrency, or informal methods

The "research only" disclaimer is a legal fig leaf. The disclaimer provides no actual legal protection to the seller under Section 29 of the Medicines Act if the product is a registerable medicine being sold without registration.

### What SAHPRA Found
SAHPRA's investigations have detected:
- **Falsified products** claiming to contain semaglutide or tirzepatide with no verified active ingredient
- **Compounded medicines** made without SAHPRA evaluation, using ingredients of unknown origin and safety
- **Substandard products** with incorrect dosing, contamination, or degraded compounds
- **Unsafe packaging** and storage conditions that compromise sterility

## How to Verify a Legitimate Peptide Source in South Africa

### The 10-Point Verification Checklist

1. **Is there an HPCSA-registered physician involved?** Ask for the physician's name and HPCSA registration number. Verify it at [hpcsa.co.za](https://www.hpcsa.co.za).
2. **Is a prescription required?** Legitimate peptide therapy requires a prescription. If you can add a vial to a cart and checkout without medical review, it is illegal and unsafe.
3. **Is the pharmacy licensed?** Ask for the name of the compounding pharmacy and its SAPC registration. Verify it.
4. **Are COAs (Certificates of Analysis) available?** A legitimate supplier provides independent third-party HPLC and MS testing for every batch.
5. **Is there a proper cold chain?** Legitimate suppliers use cold-chain shipping with tracking. Room-temperature shipping degrades the compound.
6. **Is there a physical address and contact?** A PO Box or WhatsApp-only contact is a red flag.
7. **Is there ongoing medical support?** Can you reach a physician or clinical team for questions about side effects, dose adjustments, or interactions?
8. **Are contraindications screened?** GLP-1 agonists are contraindicated in patients with certain thyroid cancers, pancreatitis, pregnancy, and specific drug interactions.
9. **Is the website on SAHPRA's warning list?** Check [sahpra.org.za](https://www.sahpra.org.za) for the latest enforcement list.
10. **Is the pricing transparent?** Legitimate clinical services publish clear pricing for consultations, prescriptions, and monthly protocols.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is it illegal to buy peptides online in South Africa?
It depends on how. Buying "research peptides" without medical oversight from a SAHPRA-warned vendor is illegal under Section 29 of the Medicines Act. Buying through a legitimate telehealth platform with physician consultation, prescription, and pharmacy fulfillment is legal and compliant.

### What happens if I bought from a named vendor?
If you have purchased from a SAHPRA-named vendor, the product you received may be falsified, substandard, or contaminated. Consult a physician before continuing use. You are not personally at criminal risk as a buyer, but you are at health risk from an unverified product.

### Are peptides legal for human use in South Africa?
Peptides classified as Schedule 4 medicines (including GLP-1 agonists) require a prescription and must be dispensed by a licensed pharmacy. The "research only" sale model circumvents these requirements and is illegal.

### Can I still get semaglutide or tirzepatide legally?
Yes. Through legitimate channels:
- **Ozempic** (semaglutide) is registered for T2D and available through licensed pharmacies with a prescription. It is not registered for weight management.
- **Mounjaro** (tirzepatide) is registered for T2D. Weight management indication is pending SAHPRA review.
- **Saxenda** (liraglutide) is the only GLP-1 registered for weight management in South Africa.
- Compounded versions through licensed compounding pharmacies with a physician prescription are the emerging legal pathway.

### What should I do if I want to start a peptide protocol?
1. Consult an HPCSA-registered physician with experience in peptide therapy
2. Complete a full medical assessment including contraindication screening
3. If clinically indicated, obtain a prescription for a specific protocol
4. Fill the prescription through a licensed compounding pharmacy
5. Follow the protocol with regular monitoring and follow-up
