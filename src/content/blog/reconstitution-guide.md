## Why Reconstitution Matters

Reconstitution is the single most error-prone step in peptide research. A simple math mistake can mean:

- **Underdosing** — No therapeutic effect, wasted time and money
- **Overdosing** — Unnecessary side effects, potential safety risks
- **Contamination** — Ruined vial, bacterial infection risk
- **Degradation** — Peptide destroyed by improper handling

This guide eliminates the guesswork.

## The Math: Concentration Calculations

### Basic Formula

```
Concentration (mg/mL) = Peptide amount (mg) / BAC water volume (mL)
Dose volume (mL) = Desired dose (mg) / Concentration (mg/mL)
```

## Step-by-Step Reconstitution

### Step 1: Prepare Your Workspace

1. Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water
2. Clean work surface with 70% isopropyl alcohol
3. Lay out all supplies within reach
4. Allow alcohol to evaporate (30 seconds)

### Step 2: Calculate Your Dilution

**Example:** You have a 5 mg BPC-157 vial and want 500 mcg doses.

```
Option A: 2 mL BAC water
- Concentration: 5 mg / 2 mL = 2.5 mg/mL
- 500 mcg dose: 0.5 mg / 2.5 mg/mL = 0.20 mL = 20 units

Option B: 1 mL BAC water
- Concentration: 5 mg / 1 mL = 5.0 mg/mL
- 500 mcg dose: 0.5 mg / 5.0 mg/mL = 0.10 mL = 10 units
```

**Recommendation:** Use 2 mL for beginners — larger volumes are easier to measure accurately.

### Step 3: Reconstitute the Peptide

1. **Wipe** the peptide vial stopper with an alcohol swab
2. **Wipe** the BAC water vial stopper with a fresh alcohol swab
3. **Draw** your calculated BAC water volume into the syringe
4. **Inject** BAC water slowly into the peptide vial:
   - Aim at the **side of the vial**, not directly onto the powder
   - Let the water run down the glass and gently wet the powder
   - **Never shake** — swirl gently if needed
5. **Do not** inject air first (unlike some medical injections)
6. **Inspect** — the solution should be clear. Slight cloudiness may occur with some peptides

### Step 4: Storage

1. **Label** the vial with:
   - Peptide name
   - Concentration (mg/mL)
   - Reconstitution date
   - Expiry date (typically +30 days)
2. **Refrigerate** immediately at 2–8°C
3. **Protect from light** — store in original box or wrap in foil
4. **Do not freeze** reconstituted peptides (destroys structure)

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Matters | How to Avoid |
|---------|---------------|--------------|
| Shaking the vial | Creates foam, may denature peptide | Swirl gently only |
| Injecting water directly onto powder | Can cause clumping, uneven dissolution | Run down side of vial |
| Using tap water | Bacterial contamination, peptide degradation | Always use BAC water |
| Not refrigerating | Peptide degrades within hours | Refrigerate immediately |
| Freezing reconstituted peptide | Ice crystals destroy peptide structure | Refrigerate only (2–8°C) |
| Using expired BAC water | Reduced bacteriostatic protection | Check expiry, use within 28 days of opening |
| Reusing needles | Contamination, dull needle pain | New needle for every injection |
| Not tracking dates | Using degraded peptide | Label everything, track in notebook |

## Advanced: Multi-Peptide Reconstitution

### When You Need Multiple Peptides

**Option 1: Separate Vials (Recommended)**
- Reconstitute each peptide in its own vial
- Draw from each vial separately
- Combine in one syringe just before injection
- Prevents chemical interactions and dosing errors

**Option 2: Pre-Mixed Vials (Advanced)**
- Some suppliers offer pre-mixed formulations
- Verify stability data for the specific combination
- Not recommended for DIY mixing unless you have lab experience

## Troubleshooting

### Cloudy Solution
- **Normal for some peptides** (e.g., GHK-Cu may have slight color)
- **Not normal:** Particulate matter, floating debris
- **Action:** If particulate, do not use. Contact supplier.

### Peptide Won't Dissolve
- Some peptides dissolve slowly
- Let sit for 5–10 minutes, then swirl gently
- Do not shake or heat
- If still undissolved after 30 minutes, contact supplier

### Vial Lost Vacuum
- When inserting needle, you should feel slight resistance (vacuum)
- If no resistance, vacuum may be compromised
- **Action:** Use immediately or discard. Compromised vials have shorter shelf life.
