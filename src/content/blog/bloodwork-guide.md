## Introduction

**Medical disclaimer:** The information in this article is for research and educational purposes only. It does not constitute medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment recommendation. Peptide compounds are not approved by SAHPRA or the FDA for many of the uses discussed here. Bloodwork interpretation requires clinical context; reference ranges vary by laboratory and population. Always consult a qualified South African healthcare professional before starting any peptide protocol, ordering bloodwork, or interpreting results. Self-diagnosis and self-treatment carry real risks. If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, have a history of cancer, diabetes, liver disease, kidney disease, or any chronic medical condition, speak to your doctor before proceeding.

If you are researching peptide protocols without a robust bloodwork monitoring plan, you are not conducting research — you are gambling with your endocrine system, metabolic health, and organ function. Bloodwork is not optional. It is the foundation of safety, the only objective measure of whether a compound is doing what you expect, and the earliest warning system for harm.

This guide covers exactly what to test, when to test it, how to interpret the results, and where to get it done in South Africa without breaking the bank. No shortcuts. No excuses.

## Why Bloodwork Is Non-Negotiable

Peptides are biologically active molecules. They bind receptors, trigger signalling cascades, and alter hormone levels, enzyme activity, and tissue repair dynamics. Some of these effects are desired. Others are collateral. You cannot feel insulin resistance developing. You cannot detect subclinical pancreatitis without a lipase test. You will not notice a gradual rise in prolactin until it produces symptoms you cannot ignore.

Bloodwork serves four critical functions:

1. **Safety screening** — Identifies pre-existing conditions that make a peptide dangerous for you. Starting a GH secretagogue with undiagnosed diabetes is reckless. Starting a GLP-1 agonist with a history of pancreatitis is dangerous.
2. **Baseline establishment** — You cannot judge whether a peptide "worked" if you do not know where you started. IGF-1, fasting glucose, inflammatory markers — all need a pre-protocol reference point.
3. **Dose calibration** — Two individuals on identical CJC-1295 + Ipamorelin doses can end up with IGF-1 levels at opposite ends of the therapeutic window. Bloodwork tells you whether to adjust, hold, or stop.
4. **Early warning** — Catching a rising HbA1c at week 6 lets you intervene before prediabetes becomes diabetes. Catching elevated liver enzymes at week 4 lets you discontinue before genuine hepatotoxicity develops.

Skipping bloodwork is not cost-saving. It is cost-shifting — from a R2,500 lab panel to a R50,000 hospital admission.

## The Core Panel: Every Protocol, Every Time

Regardless of which peptide you are researching, the following tests form the non-negotiable baseline. These evaluate organ function, metabolic health, blood composition, and systemic inflammation.

### Complete Blood Count (CBC)

| Parameter | Why It Matters | Red Flag |
|-----------|--------------|----------|
| Haemoglobin | Anaemia reduces oxygen delivery and exercise tolerance | < 12 g/dL (women), < 13 g/dL (men) |
| Haematocrit | GH secretagogues can elevate this; high Hct increases viscosity | > 50% |
| White blood cells | Infection, inflammation, or immunosuppression signal | > 11,000 or < 4,000 /µL |
| Platelets | Clotting function; some peptides affect angiogenesis | < 150,000 or > 450,000 /µL |

### Comprehensive Metabolic Panel (CMP)

| Parameter | Why It Matters | Red Flag |
|-----------|--------------|----------|
| Sodium / Potassium | Electrolyte balance; GLP-1 agonists can affect via GI losses | K+ < 3.5 or > 5.2 mmol/L |
| BUN / Creatinine | Kidney function; dehydration and some peptides stress renal clearance | eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73m² |
| Glucose (fasting) | Baseline metabolic health; secretagogues and GLP-1s both affect this | > 7.0 mmol/L (diabetic range) |
| eGFR | Overall kidney filtration capacity | Declining trend > 15% from baseline |

### Liver Function Tests (LFTs)

| Parameter | Why It Matters | Red Flag |
|-----------|--------------|----------|
| ALT | Hepatocellular injury marker | > 3× upper limit of normal |
| AST | Hepatocellular and muscle injury | > 3× upper limit of normal |
| ALP / GGT | Cholestatic injury; gallbladder issues with GLP-1s | ALP > 150 U/L; GGT > 80 U/L |
| Bilirubin | Excretion function; drug-induced liver injury | > 34 µmol/L |

### Lipid Panel

| Parameter | Why It Matters | Red Flag |
|-----------|--------------|----------|
| Total cholesterol | Cardiovascular risk baseline | > 6.2 mmol/L |
| LDL cholesterol | Atherogenic particle burden | > 4.1 mmol/L |
| HDL cholesterol | Cardioprotective; may drop on some protocols | < 1.0 mmol/L (men), < 1.3 (women) |
| Triglycerides | Metabolic health; often improve on GLP-1s | > 2.3 mmol/L |

### Inflammatory Markers

| Parameter | Why It Matters | Red Flag |
|-----------|--------------|----------|
| hs-CRP | Systemic inflammation; healing peptides should lower this | > 10 mg/L (acute), > 3 mg/L (chronic) |
| ESR | Non-specific inflammation trend | > 20 mm/hr |

**Core panel cost in South Africa:** R800–1,200 at Pathcare, Lancet, or Ampath.

## Protocol-Specific Panels

### GH Secretagogues: CJC-1295, Ipamorelin, Sermorelin, Tesamorelin

Growth hormone secretagogues increase endogenous GH release, which raises IGF-1 and alters glucose metabolism, thyroid hormone conversion, and potentially prolactin. The panel below targets these specific pathways.

| Test | Rationale | Target Range | When to Stop |
|------|-----------|--------------|--------------|
| **IGF-1** | Primary efficacy marker; reflects GH exposure | 150–250 ng/mL (age-adjusted) | > 300 ng/mL (acromegaly risk) |
| **Fasting glucose** | GH induces insulin resistance | 3.9–5.6 mmol/L | > 7.0 mmol/L or rising > 1.5 mmol/L from baseline |
| **HbA1c** | 3-month glucose control average | < 5.7% | > 6.5% |
| **Fasting insulin** | Early insulin resistance detection | < 15 µIU/mL | > 25 µIU/mL or HOMA-IR > 2.5 |
| **Prolactin** | Some GHRPs elevate this; Ipamorelin is usually clean | < 20 ng/mL (men), < 25 ng/mL (women) | > 35 ng/mL |
| **Cortisol (AM)** | GH axis interacts with HPA axis; monitor for suppression or elevation | 140–700 nmol/L (8 AM) | < 100 or > 800 nmol/L |
| **Free T3 / Free T4 / TSH** | GH increases peripheral T4→T3 conversion; may unmask subclinical hypothyroidism | TSH 0.5–4.0 mIU/L; fT3 3.5–6.5 pmol/L | TSH > 10 or fT3 < 3.0 |
| **Testosterone (men)** | GH axis and gonadal axis interact; some men report suppression | > 12 nmol/L | < 8 nmol/L with symptoms |

**GH secretagogue panel cost:** R2,000–3,500 (core + IGF-1 + thyroid + prolactin + cortisol + insulin).

**Interpretation notes:**
- IGF-1 rising from 120 to 200 ng/mL indicates the secretagogue is working. IGF-1 > 300 ng/mL is not "more gains" — it is acromegaly territory. Stop and reassess.
- Fasting glucose rising from 5.0 to 6.2 mmol/L at week 6 is a warning. Rising to 7.0 mmol/L is a stop signal. GH-induced insulin resistance is reversible if caught early.
- Prolactin elevation on Ipamorelin is rare. If it rises, verify the product is genuine and consider whether another compound is in the vial.

### GLP-1 Agonists: Semaglutide, Tirzepatide, Retatrutide

GLP-1 receptor agonists produce dramatic metabolic effects but carry specific risks: pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, severe gastroparesis, and — paradoxically — both hypoglycaemia (when combined with insulin or sulfonylureas) and hyperglycaemia rebound after discontinuation.

| Test | Rationale | Target Range | When to Stop |
|------|-----------|--------------|--------------|
| **Fasting glucose** | Primary efficacy marker for glycaemic control | 3.9–5.6 mmol/L | < 3.0 mmol/L (hypo) or > 11.1 mmol/L (hyper) |
| **HbA1c** | Long-term glycaemic control | < 5.7% (normoglycaemic), < 7.0% (diabetic target) | Rising despite therapy |
| **Lipid panel** | GLP-1s improve triglycerides and LDL; verify | LDL < 4.1 mmol/L; TG < 2.3 mmol/L | N/A — track improvement |
| **Amylase** | Pancreatic enzyme; early pancreatitis marker | 30–110 U/L | > 3× upper limit of normal |
| **Lipase** | More specific for pancreatitis than amylase | 10–140 U/L | > 3× upper limit of normal |
| **LFTs (ALT, ALP, GGT, bilirubin)** | Gallbladder stasis and bile duct obstruction risk | Within lab reference ranges | ALP or GGT > 2× baseline with RUQ pain |
| **Calcitonin** | Baseline for MTC risk screening (if family history) | < 10 pg/mL (men), < 5 pg/mL (women) | Rising trend |

**GLP-1 panel cost:** R1,500–2,500 (core + amylase + lipase + HbA1c). Add calcitonin: +R400–600.

**When to add imaging:**
- If amylase or lipase rise > 2× baseline: urgent medical review. If > 3×: stop the peptide immediately and seek emergency care.
- If you develop right upper quadrant pain, nausea after fatty meals, or pale stools: request a gallbladder ultrasound. GLP-1–mediated gallbladder stasis can produce cholelithiasis even in lean individuals [1].

**Interpretation notes:**
- HbA1c dropping from 8.5% to 6.8% in 12 weeks is excellent. HbA1c dropping below 5.0% without symptoms is not dangerous per se, but if you are on concurrent insulin or sulfonylureas, hypoglycaemia risk rises sharply.
- Fasting glucose may drop rapidly in week 1–2 before HbA1c catches up. Do not titrate doses based on fasting glucose alone in the first month.
- Lipase is more specific than amylase for pancreatitis. A lipase of 300 U/L with abdominal pain is an emergency. A lipase of 300 U/L without symptoms warrants stopping and rechecking in 48 hours.

### Healing Peptides: BPC-157, TB-500

Healing peptides operate through angiogenic, anti-inflammatory, and cellular repair mechanisms. The bloodwork goal is not to measure "healing" directly — there is no serum biomarker for tendon repair — but to ensure systemic safety and track inflammatory reduction.

| Test | Rationale | Target Range | When to Stop |
|------|-----------|--------------|--------------|
| **hs-CRP** | Systemic inflammation; should trend down | < 3 mg/L | Rising > 50% from baseline |
| **ESR** | Inflammatory trend confirmation | < 20 mm/hr | Rising > 50% from baseline |
| **CBC (platelets)** | Angiogenic peptides can theoretically affect clotting | 150–450 × 10³/µL | > 500 × 10³/µL |
| **Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF)** | Optional; BPC-157 upregulates VEGF | Baseline-dependent | Not a stop marker; research use only |
| **Tumor markers (if history)** | Theoretical concern with angiogenic compounds | Within normal limits | Any new elevation |

**Healing peptide panel cost:** R1,000–1,800 (core + inflammatory markers). VEGF is rarely available in SA private labs.

**Interpretation notes:**
- BPC-157 and TB-500 do not typically produce dramatic bloodwork changes. The absence of change in liver enzymes, kidney function, or inflammatory markers is the goal.
- If hs-CRP rises during a healing protocol, investigate other causes (infection, injury flare, poor sleep, dietary change) before blaming the peptide.
- There is no validated blood test for "wound healing rate." Track functional outcomes (pain scores, range of motion, imaging) alongside bloodwork.

### Longevity Stacks: Epitalon, FOXO4-DRI, NAD+ Precursors, Metformin

Longevity protocols are often subtle, long-term, and multifactorial. Bloodwork focuses on metabolic health, oxidative stress, and hormonal preservation.

| Test | Rationale | Target Range | When to Stop |
|------|-----------|--------------|--------------|
| **Comprehensive metabolic panel** | Organ function preservation | All within reference ranges | Any parameter > 2× baseline |
| **Lipid panel** | Cardiovascular risk reduction | LDL < 3.0 mmol/L; TG < 1.7 mmol/L | N/A |
| **Fasting insulin / HOMA-IR** | Metabolic health; mTOR modulation | HOMA-IR < 2.0 | > 3.0 |
| **Oxidative stress markers (8-OHdG, MDA)** | DNA damage and lipid peroxidation | Baseline-dependent | Rising trend |
| **Telomere length** | Optional; expensive and variable | Baseline-dependent | N/A — research marker only |
| **IGF-1** | If using Epitalon or GH-modulating peptides | Age-appropriate | > 300 ng/mL |
| **Testosterone / Estradiol** | Hormonal preservation with age | Age-appropriate ranges | Significant decline from baseline |

**Longevity panel cost:** R3,500–6,000 (full metabolic + hormones + oxidative stress). Telomere testing is not widely available in South Africa; overseas labs charge R4,000–8,000.

## Timing Schedule: When to Draw Blood

The table below provides a standardised testing schedule. Adjust based on protocol length, compound potency, and individual risk factors.

| Timepoint | Tests | Rationale |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| **Baseline (Week 0)** | Core panel + protocol-specific panel | Establish reference ranges; screen for contraindications |
| **Week 2–4 (GLP-1 only)** | Fasting glucose, amylase, lipase | Early metabolic and pancreatic safety check during titration |
| **Mid-protocol (Week 6)** | Core panel + key protocol markers | Dose calibration; catch early adverse effects |
| **End of protocol (Week 12)** | Full protocol-specific panel | Efficacy assessment; safety confirmation |
| **Maintenance (every 3 months)** | Core panel + 1–2 key markers | Ongoing monitoring for long-term protocols |
| **Post-protocol (4–6 weeks after stop)** | Core panel + key markers | Reversibility confirmation; return to baseline |

**Fasting requirements:**
- All panels require 8–12 hours fasting for glucose, insulin, HbA1c (HbA1c technically does not require fasting, but standardise by fasting anyway), and lipid panels.
- Morning cortisol must be drawn between 07:00 and 09:00.
- Testosterone (men) should be drawn between 07:00 and 10:00 for consistency.
- Hydrate well before the draw. Dehydration artificially elevates haematocrit, BUN, and creatinine.

## How to Interpret Your Results

### Reference Ranges Are Population Averages, Not Optimal Targets

Laboratory reference ranges typically represent the 2.5th to 97.5th percentile of a healthy population. Being "within range" does not mean optimal. For peptide researchers, the relevant question is: **how did this value change from my baseline, and is that change directionally consistent with the compound's known mechanisms?**

### Directional Interpretation Guide

| Marker | GH Secretagogues | GLP-1 Agonists | Healing Peptides |
|--------|------------------|----------------|------------------|
| IGF-1 | ↑ Expected | → No change | → No change |
| Fasting glucose | ↑ Watch closely | ↓ Expected | → No change |
| HbA1c | ↑ Watch closely | ↓ Expected | → No change |
| Triglycerides | → / slight ↓ | ↓ Expected | → No change |
| LDL cholesterol | → | ↓ Expected | → No change |
| hs-CRP | → / slight ↓ | ↓ Expected | ↓ Expected |
| Prolactin | → / slight ↑ (Ipamorelin minimal) | → No change | → No change |
| Cortisol | → | → No change | → No change |
| Liver enzymes | → | → / slight ↑ (gallbladder) | → No change |
| Kidney function | → | → / slight ↓ (dehydration) | → No change |

### When to Stop: Hard Rules

Stop the protocol and seek medical review if any of the following occur:

1. **IGF-1 > 300 ng/mL** on any GH secretagogue protocol
2. **Fasting glucose > 7.0 mmol/L** or **HbA1c > 6.5%** and rising
3. **Lipase or amylase > 3× upper limit of normal**
4. **ALT or AST > 3× upper limit of normal**
5. **eGFR decline > 20% from baseline**
6. **Haematocrit > 52%** (GH secretagogues increase RBC mass)
7. **Prolactin > 35 ng/mL** with symptoms (galactorrhoea, libido loss, mood disturbance)
8. **Any new cardiac arrhythmia, chest pain, or severe shortness of breath**

These are not suggestions. They are stop signals. Ignoring them converts manageable side effects into permanent damage.

## Red Flags Requiring Immediate Medical Attention

Some symptoms demand same-day medical evaluation, not a wait-and-see approach:

| Symptom | Possible Cause | Action |
|---------|--------------|--------|
| Severe abdominal pain radiating to back | Pancreatitis (GLP-1 agonists) | Emergency department immediately |
| Right upper quadrant pain + fever + jaundice | Cholecystitis / cholangitis | Emergency department immediately |
| Severe persistent vomiting with dehydration | Gastroparesis / bowel obstruction | Urgent care or emergency department |
| Chest pain, palpitations, dyspnoea | Cardiac event (rare; consider electrolyte disturbance) | Emergency department immediately |
| Severe headache, visual changes, confusion | Hypertensive emergency; pituitary apoplexy (rare) | Emergency department immediately |
| Unilateral leg swelling + pain | Deep vein thrombosis (theoretical angiogenic risk) | Emergency department immediately |
| Black/tarry stools or vomiting blood | GI bleeding (GLP-1–associated gastritis/ulcer) | Emergency department immediately |

Do not rationalise these symptoms. Do not post in forums asking for opinions. Go to the emergency department. Bring your bloodwork results and a list of everything you are using.

## South African Lab Options: Where to Test

South Africa has three major private pathology chains with national coverage. All accept self-referrals for most routine bloodwork. You do not need a doctor's script for CBC, CMP, lipids, IGF-1, HbA1c, or thyroid panels. Some specialised tests (calcitonin, telomere length, certain hormone assays) may require a referring practitioner.

### Pathcare

- **Coverage:** 50+ collection centres nationwide; major presence in Gauteng, Western Cape, KwaZulu-Natal, Eastern Cape
- **Booking:** Online booking system at pathcare.co.za; walk-ins accepted at most centres
- **Turnaround:** 24–72 hours for routine panels; IGF-1 and specialised tests 3–5 days
- **Pricing:** Competitive; frequent promotional bundles. Basic panel R800–1,000. IGF-1 alone R450–650.
- **Pros:** Largest network; online results portal; mobile app
- **Cons:** Busy centres in major metros can have long queues before 09:00

### Lancet Laboratories

- **Coverage:** 100+ collection centres; strong in Gauteng, North West, Limpopo, Mpumalanga
- **Booking:** Online at lancet.co.za; corporate and individual bookings accepted
- **Turnaround:** 24–48 hours for routine; 3–5 days for specialised endocrine markers
- **Pricing:** Slightly higher than Pathcare for some panels. Basic panel R900–1,100. Comprehensive endocrine panel R2,200–3,000.
- **Pros:** Excellent customer service; comfortable collection centres; good paediatric phlebotomy
- **Cons:** Fewer Western Cape and KZN locations than Pathcare

### Ampath

- **Coverage:** 100+ collection centres; strongest in Gauteng, Western Cape, KwaZulu-Natal, Free State
- **Booking:** Online at ampath.co.za; walk-ins accepted
- **Turnaround:** 24–48 hours routine; 3–7 days for specialised tests
- **Pricing:** Mid-range. Basic panel R850–1,050. Full hormone + metabolic panel R2,500–3,500.
- **Pros:** Integrated with many hospital groups; strong academic pathology backing; reliable specialised testing
- **Cons:** Some centres have limited weekend hours

### How to Book

1. Visit the lab's website and select "Book a Test" or "Patient Services"
2. Choose your tests or select a pre-defined panel (wellness, hormone, metabolic)
3. Select a collection centre and time slot
4. Pay online or at the centre
5. Fast 8–12 hours before your appointment; drink water
6. Bring your ID and booking reference
7. Results are typically emailed or available via patient portal within 24–72 hours

**Money-saving tips:**

- Do not split tests across labs. Reference ranges differ slightly between laboratories, and comparing results across different analysers introduces noise.

## Tracking Results Over Time: Build Your Spreadsheet

Bloodwork is only useful if you can see trends. A single snapshot tells you little. A time series tells you everything. Build a simple tracking spreadsheet with the following structure:

| Date | Protocol | Dose | IGF-1 | Fasting Glucose | HbA1c | LDL | TG | hs-CRP | ALT | eGFR | Notes |
|------|----------|------|-------|-----------------|-------|-----|-----|--------|-----|------|-------|
| 2026-01-15 | Baseline | None | 125 | 5.1 | 5.2% | 3.8 | 1.4 | 2.1 | 22 | 95 | Pre-protocol |
| 2026-03-01 | CJC+Ipam | 100/100 mcg | 198 | 5.4 | 5.3% | 3.6 | 1.2 | 1.8 | 24 | 93 | Week 6 mid-test |
| 2026-04-15 | CJC+Ipam | 100/100 mcg | 215 | 5.6 | 5.4% | 3.5 | 1.1 | 1.5 | 21 | 94 | Week 12 end-test |
| 2026-06-01 | Off | None | 140 | 5.2 | 5.2% | 3.7 | 1.3 | 1.9 | 23 | 95 | Post-protocol |

**Spreadsheet rules:**
- Use the same lab for every draw to keep reference ranges consistent
- Record exact dose, time of last injection, and fasting duration in the Notes column
- Flag any value > 20% changed from baseline in yellow; > 50% or outside reference range in red
- Share this spreadsheet with your healthcare provider at every consultation

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I skip bloodwork if I feel fine?**

No. Many peptide side effects are asymptomatic in early stages. Insulin resistance, subclinical liver enzyme elevation, and rising prolactin do not produce noticeable symptoms until they are advanced. Feeling fine is not a biomarker.

**What if my medical aid does not cover these tests?**

Pay out of pocket. The cost of a comprehensive panel (R2,500–3,500) is trivial compared to the cost of managing diabetes, pancreatitis, or liver injury. Consider it insurance, not an expense.

**Can I use a home glucose monitor instead of lab fasting glucose?**

A home glucometer is useful for daily tracking during GLP-1 titration or GH secretagogue use, but it is not a substitute for lab testing. Home meters have ±15% accuracy variance and cannot measure HbA1c, lipase, liver enzymes, or IGF-1. Use them as supplementary data only.

**How do I explain these tests to my doctor?**

Be honest. Tell your doctor you are researching peptide compounds and want to monitor safety markers. Most general practitioners will order CBC, CMP, lipids, and glucose without issue. If they are unfamiliar with IGF-1 or specific peptide monitoring, show them this guide. If they refuse, find another doctor. A doctor who discourages monitoring is not a doctor you want.

**Should I test during a peptide break or off-cycle?**

Yes. Post-protocol testing (4–6 weeks after stopping) confirms reversibility and establishes a clean baseline for the next cycle. If values have not returned to baseline, investigate why before restarting.

**What about hair mineral analysis, live blood analysis, or other alternative tests?**

Do not waste your money. These tests lack scientific validity, standardised reference ranges, and clinical utility. Stick to evidence-based laboratory medicine: Pathcare, Lancet, or Ampath.
